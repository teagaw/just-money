Her left hand was flat on the crate. A fish scale stuck to the heel of it. She left it there.

Sable closed the ledger. "Second tide bell," she said, as though Maren were slow. "Warden Row. Ask for me."

"Heard."

"Then we understand each other."

"Do we."

Sable looked at her. Rain ran off the hat brim in a clean line. "Constable."

The young one stopped writing. "Warden."

"Note the hour. And the weather."

"Already have."

"Good." Sable turned. Two steps. Then she stopped. "Vosk."

Maren waited.

"One more thing." A pause. Not long. Long enough. "The writ is not the only page in this city with your name on it. There is a second. I would prefer to discuss it with you in a room with a door."

She walked. The constable followed. At the end of the row he looked back. At Tovin. Not at Maren.

Then Tovin.

"Look," Tovin said. "Look, Maren, I didn't— I mean, I did, but not on purpose, and I said I was sorry, and she said it was fine, and then she wrote it down, and—"

"Tovin."

"—and I only told her what I saw, which was nothing, mostly nothing, and she had that book, and I thought if I kept talking she'd go away, and—"

"Tovin."

He stopped. His mouth stayed open.

"What did you tell her."

"About the job. The woman. The green shawl. I said a woman offered you work and you said no, and then she asked what kind of work, and I said I didn't know, which is true, I don't, and then—" He trailed off. "I said you said no."

"Good."

"Is it?"

"Means she doesn't know what the job was." Maren flexed her left hand. The scale came off and stuck to her palm. "Means she's guessing."

"Look, we could go. My cousin has a boat. Tide Isles. Two days and we're gone, nobody finds—"

"Nobody finds you. You owe ten marks."

"I'll pay it! I'll— I have six. Six and a half. There's a man at the Salt Market who buys teeth, and I have all of mine, and—"

"Sit down."

He sat down on a crate. He apologized to the crate.

Maren looked at the row. Third stall. Rope-seller. The woman in the green shawl was still there. She had been there the whole time. She had watched all of it.

She was watching Maren now.

Maren raised two fingers. Not a wave. Just enough.

The woman did not move. Then she did. She set something on the rope-seller's table — a coin, or a button — and walked into the crowd toward the harbor steps.

Tovin said, "Who's that."

"Client."

"Which client? The— look, Maren, if she's the one who—"

"Stay."

"I'm staying. I'm staying right here. I'm not moving. I'm—" He was already standing.

Maren watched the green shawl go. Forty paces to the harbor steps. Then the crowd took her.

Second tide bell. Maren did not own a clock. She had a feeling about the light, and the light was going.

She could go after the woman. She could go to Warden Row and ask for Sable, and hear about the second page. She could put Tovin on a boat and be rid of him.

The rain came off the awning seam again. It found her shoulder.

"Stay here," Maren said. "Count to two hundred. If we're not back, go to your cousin."

"Where are you—"

But she was already moving.

The rope-seller was a wide man with tar under his nails. He did not look up. "You buying or bleeding."

"Neither." Maren put a silver mark on the table. "What'd she leave."

He looked at the coin. He looked at her. He took the coin and made it gone. "Writ. Sealed. Said a woman in a grey coat would come for it. Said don't give it to Wardens."

"Did she say why."

"Said the seal's worth more than the paper." He shrugged. "I don't read. Bad for the eyes."

Maren picked it up. Heavy paper, good paper. Council stock. The seal was a blob of red wax with a wren pressed into it.

She knew that wren. She had seen it once before, on a door in the old quarter, the night her sister did not come home.

Tovin's voice came from behind her, thin over the rain. "Maren? Maren, I counted to forty and I don't like it, I don't like any of this, look—"

The harbor steps went down into the crowd. Green shawl, forty paces, gone.

Warden Row was the other way. Ten minutes, uphill, dry under the colonnade.

The tide bell was not far off.
