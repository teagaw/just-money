Rain fell hard on Duskport. It filled the gutters and turned the Salt Market stones black. It drummed on the canvas awnings. Maren stood under one. Water leaked through a seam and tapped her shoulder. She did not move.

Tovin moved. He shifted from foot to foot. His coat was too thin. His hands were empty, then full of a folded paper, then empty again.

"She is late," he said. "Not that she is not coming. She is always late. Or she was. Before... look, I checked the tide board. The ferry from the Reach docked an hour ago. If she took it, she should be here. Unless she did not take it. Which is also possible. Sorry."

Maren watched the crowd. "We wait."

"Of course. We wait. I like waiting. It gives a person time to think about all the things that could go wrong..."

"Stop."

Tovin closed his mouth. He nodded too many times.

Maren's sister had sent one message in three weeks. Three words on oilcloth. Salt Market. Low tide. No signature. Maren had kept the scrap until the ink ran. Now it was ash in a bowl at home. She did not need it. She knew the words.

A bell rang from the harbor. The crowd thinned. Fishwives covered their baskets. A beggar dragged a crate of rotten oysters toward the seawall. Then the Wardens came.

They came in pairs. Gray cloaks dark with rain. People stepped back without being told. The Wardens did not hurry. At their head walked a woman with silver at her throat and a ledger under her arm. Warden Sable.

"Stay," Maren said.

Sable stopped before the awning. Her eyes moved over Tovin, then Maren. Rain ran from the brim of her hat. She did not blink it away.

"Maren Holt," she said.

Maren kept her hands in her pockets. "Warden."

Sable opened the ledger. The pages were dry. She had kept them under her cloak. "There was a body in the north quarter this morning. A courier. He carried a sealed writ from the Merchant Council. The writ is missing."

Tovin swallowed. "That is terrible. I mean, a courier. Dead. In this weather. It does not seem right."

Sable did not look at him. "No. It does not."

Maren said nothing.

Sable turned a page. "The courier was seen entering the Salt Market before dawn. He asked for a woman named Maren."

Tovin's breath caught. Maren did not move.

"Did you meet him?" Sable asked.

"No," Maren said.

Sable studied her. "You are certain."

"Yes."

Sable closed the ledger. "Certainty is a poor shield, Holt. It breaks when struck." She paused. "I will ask again. Did you meet the courier?"

Tovin stepped forward. "She did not. I was with her. We were here since dawn, buying eels. Well, looking at eels. The eel man was very rude about it, but that is not the point. The point is, we did not meet any courier. I would remember a courier. They have a look, do they not? Boots, satchel, that sort of... sorry."

Sable looked at him at last. "You are her friend."

"Yes."

"Then you will understand this." Sable lifted the ledger. "Harboring a courier's secret is theft. Lying to a Warden is obstruction. Obstruction carries a fine. The fine is ten silver marks. Or three days in the barracks."

"It is not a misunderstanding," Sable said. "It is a choice."

Maren looked past her. The crowd had formed a loose ring. No one would help. No one would interfere. At the edge of the market, two Wardens blocked the alley. Another stood by the fish stalls. The rain kept falling.

Sable held out her hand. "The writ, if you have it. If you do not, you will come with me."

Tovin looked at Maren. "Maren..."

Sable's voice stayed even. "I am not asking twice."
