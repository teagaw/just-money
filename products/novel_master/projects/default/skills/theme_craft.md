When writing or reviewing themes:
- The controlling idea: what is the story really about? (not the plot, the meaning)
- Themes should be woven in through action and character, not stated
- Motifs: recurring images, objects, or phrases that carry thematic weight
- Avoid: characters stating the theme explicitly ("The real treasure was friendship")
- Each major character should embody a different aspect of the theme
- Theme should emerge from conflict, not from narration
- Subtext: what the characters don't say is as important as what they do say
