# Mockup Prompts — 5 Listing Gallery Images

The mockups are the supporting images inside the listing, after the hero cover. They give the buyer confidence the bundle is real, complete, and works in real-world contexts. Five is the right number for a marketplace listing — more than five and the buyer stops scrolling; fewer than five and the bundle feels thin.

Each mockup answers a different question the buyer has while evaluating:

1. **Mockup 1 — Hero shot of the cover.** "What does the bundle look like as a product?"
2. **Mockup 2 — Phone in hand with IG feed open.** "What will my actual Instagram look like?"
3. **Mockup 3 — Laptop with Canva editor showing a template.** "How do I actually use it?"
4. **Mockup 4 — Grid of all 50 posts laid out.** "What's actually inside? Show me everything."
5. **Mockup 5 — Day in the life of a small biz owner using the bundle.** "What does success with this bundle look like?"

Each prompt is provided in **Midjourney v6** format (with parameters) and **DALL-E 3** format (natural language). Use whichever model you're running. The prompts are designed to produce clean, premium-feeling results that match the warm editorial direction of `cover-prompt.md`.

**The question it answers:** "What will my actual Instagram feed look like when I use this?"

**Composition:** A real-looking hand (not a stock-photo one) holding a phone, scrolling an Instagram feed that shows several of the bundle's templates in a row. The viewer should be able to see 3-4 of the bundle's designs in the feed at once.

### Where to use it

- **Etsy listing gallery, image 2.** This is the second-most-important image in the listing. Buyers want to see "what will my feed look like?"
- **Instagram ad creative.** This prompt produces image-ad-ready material.
- **Email marketing** in the "what success looks like" section of the welcome sequence.

### Important notes

- **The "no face" constraint is critical.** A face in the mockup makes the buyer feel like the image is selling to her as a model, not as a customer. Keep the face out of frame.
- **The hand must look real.** Most AI image models default to either mannequin hands or stock-photo "professional hand" hands. Add "natural skin texture, slight imperfections, no manicure" if needed. Midjourney v6 with `--style raw` usually handles this well.
- **The Instagram feed content.** The image models can't render the templates perfectly. Composite the actual feed in post — take a screenshot of a curated Instagram profile and mask it onto the phone screen. This is the single most important post-production step for this mockup.

**The question it answers:** "How do I actually use this in Canva? What does the editing experience look like?"

**Composition:** A laptop (MacBook, ideally) on a desk, the screen showing the Canva editor with one of the bundle's templates open and being edited. The viewer should be able to see the editor UI around the template, signaling "this is editable software, not a finished file."

### Where to use it

- **Etsy listing gallery, image 3.** The "how to use" image.
- **Tutorial content** in the welcome email sequence.
- **Blog post** about the bundle.

### Important notes

- **Canva UI accuracy.** The Canva editor looks like a specific thing. The image models will approximate, not replicate exactly. That's fine — the buyer doesn't need pixel-perfect Canva UI. She needs to see "laptop, editor, template, my desk" energy.
- **The template in the editor should be one of the bundle's actual designs.** If you can, screenshot a real edit in progress on the actual template, then mask it onto the laptop screen in Photoshop/Canva. This is the second-most-important post-production step (after Mockup 2's feed screenshot).
- **The MacBook angle.** Make sure the screen is angled enough that the buyer can see the design, but not so angled that the screen is unreadable. 30-45 degrees is the sweet spot.

## Mockup 4 — Grid of All 50 Posts Laid Out

**The question it answers:** "What's actually inside? Show me everything."

**Composition:** All 50 templates (or as many as will fit) arranged in a grid on a flat surface, like a brand identity guide or a designer's portfolio. The viewer should be able to see the variety of layouts, the cohesion of the palette, and the volume of content.

### Midjourney v6

```
Instagram post templates laid out in a 5x10 grid on a large warm cream
linen surface, the templates together form a cohesive collection in a

sage green, and bone white, the variety of layouts is visible --
quote posts, testimonial posts, product posts, tip carousels, and
behind-the-scenes posts all arranged together, the designs feature
elegant serif display typography alongside clean sans-serif body
type, overhead top-down camera angle, soft natural daylight, the
mood is like a designer's portfolio flat-lay, premium product
photography, 4:5 aspect ratio --ar 4:5 --v 6 --style raw --s 200
```

### DALL-E 3

```
A premium product mockup: a complete collection of fifty square
Instagram post templates laid out in a 5-by-10 grid on a large piece
of warm cream linen fabric. The fifty templates together form a
cohesive collection in a unified warm editorial palette of cream, ink
black, soft terracotta, sage green, and bone white. The variety of
layouts is visible — quote posts, testimonial posts, product posts,
tip carousels, and behind-the-scenes posts are all arranged together
in the grid. The designs feature elegant serif display typography
alongside clean sans-serif body type. Overhead top-down camera angle.
Soft natural daylight. The mood is like a designer's portfolio flat
lay. Premium product photography. 4:5 aspect ratio.
```

### Where to use it

- **Etsy listing gallery, image 4.** The "all 50 posts" image.
- **Pinterest pin** for "instagram template bundle" searches.
- **Gumroad product gallery**.

### Important notes

- **AI image models cannot render 50 unique templates.** The grids they produce will look repetitive or generic. This is a hard limit.
- **The right way to do this:** Generate the 50 templates yourself in Canva, then composite them in Photoshop or Canva. Take a photo of the result, or screenshot the Canva grid view.
- **Alternative:** Use a "sampled" version. Show 15-20 of the templates in the grid, not all 50. The buyer gets the variety message without you having to render 50 unique designs in an AI tool.
- **The most authentic version:** Print the templates, lay them on a real surface, photograph them. The imperfections (slight paper weight, slight angle differences) are exactly what makes it look real.

### Hybrid approach (recommended)

Use this prompt to generate the *surface* and *environment* (linen, lighting, oak, props), then in Photoshop/Canva:

1. Mask the linen surface from the AI-generated image.
2. Place your 15-20 actual template renders in a grid on top.
3. Add a soft drop shadow under each.
4. Composite back onto the original linen surface.

This gives you the AI-generated environment with the real templates. Best of both worlds.

### Where to use it

- **Etsy listing gallery, image 5.** The "lifestyle" image — the aspirational closer.
- **Pinterest pin** for "small business owner aesthetic" searches.
- **Instagram ad creative** for cold audiences.
- **Email sequence** as the "this could be you" image.

### Important notes

- **This is the only mockup with a person.** The face should be soft, present, not the focus. A side profile or a soft-focus 3/4 view works best. Full-face portraits in mockup ads feel aggressive.
- **The "props tell the story" approach.** The printed calendar, the notebook with captions, the phone showing the feed — these are the bundle's deliverables, but they need to feel like *her* deliverables, not staged props. Add a few "her own" items: a coffee mug with a personal sticker, a small framed photo, a book she's reading. The mix signals "real life, not a stock photo."
- **The mood is the message.** Buyers want to feel "this could be me on a Tuesday morning." If the mood is "glamorous, expensive, unattainable," you've lost. If the mood is "calm, capable, real," you've won.
- **AI hands and faces.** The "no face visible" rule from Mockup 2 doesn't apply here — we want a face. But the AI models still struggle with hands. If the model's hands look wrong, prompt for "hands resting on the laptop keyboard naturally" or "one hand on the coffee mug, the other on the desk."

## Post-Production Pipeline

For each mockup, expect to do 15-30 minutes of post-production in Canva or Photoshop. The most common adjustments:

1. **Mask the phone/laptop screen with the actual app content.** Take a screenshot, mask it onto the screen in the AI-generated image. The screen content is the most important part of the mockup — it has to look real.
2. **Adjust the color grading.** AI-generated images often drift slightly cool or slightly oversaturated. Pull the warmth up by +5-10 and drop saturation by -5 to match the bundle's palette.
3. **Add a subtle vignette.** A dark, soft vignette around the edges focuses the eye on the center. Just 5-10% opacity.
4. **Remove artifacts.** AI-generated images sometimes have small anomalies (a strange shadow, a duplicated object, a hand with six fingers). Spot-fix these in Photoshop's healing brush or Canva's magic eraser.
5. **Resize and export.** Final size: 1080 × 1350 px (4:5 aspect ratio). Export as PNG, highest quality.

### Recommended exports

- **PNG** for the marketplace listing (best quality, larger file size)
- **JPG** for ads and Pinterest (smaller file size, fast load)
- **PDF** for any "lookbook" or "press kit" use

## Final Notes

Generate 3-5 candidates for each mockup. Pick the one where the *mood* is right, not just the composition. The bundle sells because of the warm editorial direction — the mood carries that. A technically correct mockup with cold, sterile lighting will underperform a slightly imperfect mockup with the right warmth.

The buyer doesn't need to be able to articulate "this mockup has the right color grading." She just needs to *feel* premium. Let your eye pick the one that feels right. That instinct is the product.
