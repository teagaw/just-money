# Caption Templates — 30 Fill-in-the-Blank Captions

Every caption in this file follows the same three-part structure:

> **HOOK** (line 1) — stops the thumb
> **BODY** (paragraph) — delivers the value, story, or context
> **CTA** (line) — tells the reader what to do next

The `[BRACKETS]` are fill-in placeholders for the buyer. Everything else is copy the buyer keeps and rewrites. All 30 templates are designed to be saved as separate docs the buyer can keep in a swipe file.

Each template comes with:
- A label (so the buyer can find it)
- The full structure
- A worked example (italicized, showing the format filled in)
- A note on *when* to use it

The 30 templates break down as 10 educational, 10 promotional, 10 engagement. This matches the post category distribution in the calendar.

## Part 1 — Educational Captions (10)

These captions turn the buyer's tips, carousels, and "how to" posts into scroll-stopping lessons.

### E1 — The Counterintuitive Take

The "everyone believes X, but actually Y" caption. Highest save rate in the educational category.

**Structure:**
> [Counterintuitive one-liner. State the wrong belief first, then flip it.]
>
> I've been [doing the thing the wrong way] for [time period].
> It took [trigger — a client, a number, a moment] to see it.
>
> The thing no one tells you: [the actual insight].
>
> The result: [the outcome, the new approach, the lesson].
>
> If you've been [committing the same mistake], try [the alternative]. It's not the [obvious fix], but it's the one that works.
>
> → Save this for the next time you [trigger situation].

**Worked example:**
> *You don't need more followers. You need better ones.*
>
> *I spent two years chasing 10K. Then I spent six months emailing my actual customers and tripled my revenue.*
>
> *The thing no one tells you: bigger audience ≠ better business. The right 200 followers will outspend the wrong 10,000 every single time.*
>
> *The result: I now spend 80% of my energy on retention, not reach.*
>
> *If you've been obsessing over follower count, try emailing your last 10 customers this week. Ask them what they wish you'd make next. It's not the vanity metric, but it's the one that pays.*
>
> *→ Save this for the next time you're tempted to buy followers.*

### E2 — The Numbered List Drop

For "5 things I learned" or "3 mistakes to avoid" carousels. Direct, scannable, saveable.

**Structure:**
> [Number] [things] [context — that I wish I'd known / that changed everything for me].
>
> [Numbered list — 3 to 7 items, each one line.]
>
> Which one do you need to hear most right now? Tell me in the comments — I read every one.
>
> → Save this post. Come back to it on [trigger day — Monday, Sunday, the next time you ___].

**Worked example:**
> *5 pricing mistakes I made in my first year — so you don't have to.*
>
> *1. Pricing by the hour instead of by the outcome.*
> *2. Discounting to "be competitive" (it killed my margins).*
> *3. Not charging for revisions.*
> *4. Saying yes to every scope creep.*
> *5. Hiding my price behind "DM me for a quote."*
>
> *Which one do you need to hear most right now? Tell me in the comments — I read every one.*
>
> *→ Save this post. Come back to it the next time you're about to write a proposal.*

### E3 — The "What I Wish I Knew"

A retrospective caption. The voice of experience. Perfect for behind-the-scenes posts that pivot into a lesson.

**Structure:**
> [Time hook — "A year ago today," "When I started," "5 years in."]
>
> [Set the scene. What was happening in the business?]
>
> Here's what I wish someone had told me: [the lesson, in one sentence].
>
> [Expand the lesson in 2-3 sentences. Add a specific example or number.]
>
> If you're in this season — [acknowledgement of the reader's situation] — just keep going. [The reassurance / the truth.]
>
> → Follow for more honest takes on [topic]. New post every [day].

**Worked example:**
> *A year ago today, I almost quit.*
>
> *I had three clients, no pipeline, and a Squarespace site I was embarrassed to share. I told my partner I was going to "get a real job."*
>
> *Here's what I wish someone had told me: the slow months are the foundation. They feel like failure, but they're actually the soil.*
>
> *I closed two of my best-ever clients the month after I almost quit. Neither came from a cold pitch — both came from a DM reply to a post I'd almost deleted.*
>
> *If you're in this season — staring at the numbers, doubting the work — just keep going. The market is bigger than your slowest month.*
>
> *→ Follow for more honest takes on building a one-person business. New post every Tuesday.*

### E4 — The "How To" Mini-Tutorial

A 3-step how-to in caption form. Works best with a carousel.

**Structure:**
> How to [outcome] in [time period] — without [the thing people think is required].
>
> Step 1: [action]. [One sentence of context.]
> Step 2: [action]. [One sentence of context.]
> Step 3: [action]. [One sentence of context.]
>
> The trick: [the non-obvious part. The thing most people miss.]
>
> → Save this. Try it this week. Then tell me how it went.

**Worked example:**
> *How to raise your prices without losing a single client — in 3 emails.*
>
> *Step 1: Email your current clients 30 days before the increase. Tell them what's changing and why. Lead with the value you've added since your last price change.*
>
> *Step 2: Email the new pricing to new leads. Use the same language — value first, price second.*
>
> *Step 3: Hold the line. The clients who push back are usually the ones who were going to churn anyway.*
>
> *The trick: don't apologize. The minute you say "I know this is a lot" you've already lost.*
>
> *→ Save this. Try it this week. Then tell me how it went.*

### E5 — The Myth-Bust Caption

For myth-vs-truth carousels or single posts. Strong opinion, generous proof.

**Structure:**
> Myth: [the wrong belief, stated confidently].
> Truth: [the correct belief, stated confidently].
>
> I used to [do the wrong thing]. It cost me [specific cost — money, time, clients, sleep].
>
> Then I tried [the right thing]. [Specific result, with a number if possible.]
>
> The takeaway: [one sentence that lands the lesson].
>
> → Save this. Send it to someone who needs to hear it.

**Worked example:**
> *Myth: "I need to be on every social platform."*
> *Truth: "I need to be excellent on one."*
>
> *I used to post the same content to Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, Pinterest, and Twitter. It took 12 hours a week and moved my revenue exactly zero.*
>
> *Then I quit everything except Instagram. I went from 12 hours to 2, and my revenue doubled in 6 months.*
>
> *The takeaway: depth beats distribution. Always has.*
>
> *→ Save this. Send it to a friend who's about to launch on a fifth platform.*

### E6 — The "Here It Is, Free"

A value drop. Buyer gives away the playbook in the caption. Builds reciprocity and trust.

**Structure:**
> Here's [the thing] — completely free. No opt-in, no gate.
>
> [Body — explain what the thing is, who it's for, and how to use it. Be specific.]
>
> [Why you're giving it away: a belief, a story, a "I wish someone had given this to me."]
>
> → Save this post. Share it with one person who's [in the situation the thing solves].

**Worked example:**
> *Here's my exact onboarding email sequence — completely free. No opt-in, no gate.*
>
> *It's 4 emails over 7 days. Email 1: the welcome. Email 2: the story behind the brand. Email 3: the social proof. Email 4: the soft pitch.*
>
> *Why I'm giving it away: I spent 3 years and $4,000 testing welcome sequences. The version I'm sharing converts at 18%. You should have it for free.*
>
> *→ Save this post. Share it with one person who just launched something.*

### E7 — The Frame-By-Frame Carousel Walkthrough

A caption that walks the viewer through the carousel they're swiping.

**Structure:**
> [Swipe →] Here's what's coming:
>
> Slide 1: [what's on it]
> Slide 2: [what's on it]
> Slide 3: [what's on it]
> Slide 4: [what's on it]
> Slide 5: [what's on it]
>
> [The big takeaway in one sentence.]
>
> → Save the whole thing. Send it to a friend who [needs it].

**Worked example:**
> *[Swipe →] Here's what's coming:*
>
> *Slide 1: The pricing mistake that's quietly costing you clients.*
> *Slide 2: The "value-based" framework in plain English.*
> *Slide 3: The exact sentence to add to your next proposal.*
> *Slide 4: The "no" script for scope creep.*
> *Slide 5: The one metric to track this month.*
>
> *The big takeaway: pricing is a system, not a number.*
>
> *→ Save the whole thing. Send it to a friend who's about to send a proposal.*

### E8 — The "Steal This" Caption

A direct giveaway. "Here's the exact thing I use." The "I" can be the buyer.

**Structure:**
> Steal this: [the thing — a script, a sequence, a framework, a tool, a link].
>
> [What it does, in one sentence.]
> [Why it works, in one sentence.]
> [How to use it, in one sentence.]
>
> [Optional: a small confession or context — "I've been using it for 2 years and I'm only just now sharing it."]
>
> → Copy. Paste. Use today. Don't @ me. (Just kidding, @ me — I want to see.)

**Worked example:**
> *Steal this: my pre-call questionnaire. The one I send to every prospect before a discovery call.*
>
> *It cuts my discovery calls from 45 minutes to 20, increases my close rate by 30%, and makes the prospect feel heard before we even talk.*
>
> *I'll link it in stories all week. Use it. Edit it. Make it yours.*
>
> *→ Copy. Paste. Use today. Don't @ me. (Just kidding, @ me — I want to see.)*

### E9 — The "Two Lists" Caption

A "do this / stop that" caption. Built for the Two-Column Comparison carousel (C3).

**Structure:**
> [Number] things to stop doing this [week / month / quarter]:
>
> [Stop list — 3-5 items, each one line, with a one-line context per item.]
>
> [Number] things to start doing instead:
>
> [Start list — 3-5 items, each one line, with a one-line context per item.]
>
> The shift is small. The compounding is real.
>
> → Save this. Pick one to start, one to stop. Report back.

**Worked example:**
> *3 things to stop doing this quarter:*
>
> *1. Posting to Instagram every day "to stay visible." (Quality > frequency. Always.)*
> *2. Saying yes to every custom request. (Your "yes" is your roadmap. Protect it.)*
> *3. Discounting to win the deal. (Discounting attracts the wrong customers.)*
>
> *3 things to start doing instead:*
>
> *1. Batching content on Sundays. (2 hours, all month.)*
> *2. Raising prices 15% on new leads. (No apology, no "I know this is a lot.")*
> *3. Replying to every DM within 24 hours. (Even the awkward ones.)*
>
> *The shift is small. The compounding is real.*
>
> *→ Save this. Pick one to start, one to stop. Report back.*

### E10 — The "Long Read" Caption

A 6-10 sentence story that ends in a takeaway. For behind-the-scenes and personal posts that double as lessons.

**Structure:**
> [Open with a scene — a moment, a quote, a number.]
>
> [The setup — what was happening, who was involved, what the stakes were.]
>
> [The turn — what changed, what broke, what someone said.]
>
> [The resolution — what you did, what you learned, what the new normal is.]
>
> [The lesson, stated cleanly in one sentence.]
>
> [Optional: a small "you" bridge to the reader.]
>
> → Save this for the next time [trigger situation].

**Worked example:**
> *Last Tuesday, a customer emailed to say my $22 candle was "too expensive."*
>
> *I've heard this four times in two years. Every single time, the customer who said it went on to become a repeat buyer.*
>
> *The first time it happened, I almost lowered the price. The second time, I almost apologized. The third time, I started to wonder if I was the problem.*
>
> *The fourth time — last week — I just replied: "Thanks for the feedback. The price reflects the [specific materials / time / process]. If it's not in your budget right now, no hard feelings."*
>
> *She bought two.*
>
> *The lesson: the customers who complain about price are usually the ones who will pay it. The ones who ghost you never had intent to begin with.*
>
> *If you've been agonizing over a price increase, this is your sign.*
>
> *→ Save this for the next time someone tells you your thing is "too expensive."*

## Part 2 — Promotional Captions (10)

These captions turn product promos, launches, and offers into sales-driving posts. They never read as "salesy" because the value comes first.

### P1 — The Soft Launch

For new product drops. Creates anticipation without screaming "BUY NOW."

**Structure:**
> [Time anchor — "A year ago," "Last winter," "Two prototypes ago."]
> I started making [product] because [the gap I kept finding].
>
> Today it's finally [status: live / here / yours].
>
> [What's special about it — 2-3 details. Materials, design choice, the thing no one else is doing.]
>
> [The offer: a price, a link, a "link in bio."]
>
> → First [number] orders get [the bonus — a freebie, a personalization, early access to the next colorway]. Comment [emoji or word] if you want one.

**Worked example:**
> *A year ago, I was carrying a tote that fell apart in 3 months.*
>
> *Every tote I tried was either flimsy, ugly, or lined with plastic that made the inside smell like a swimming pool. So I started making my own.*
>
> *Today the Linen Tote is finally live. 12 oz natural canvas, leather handles, machine-washable liner, and a hidden zip pocket for your keys.*
>
> *$58. Link in bio.*
>
> *→ First 30 orders get a free leather keychain. Comment "TOTE" if you want one.*

### P2 — The Sale Announcement

For promotional offers. The structure protects against sounding desperate.

**Structure:**
> [Sale name or context — "Spring sale," "Black Friday," "End-of-season"].
>
> [Time bound — "48 hours," "this weekend," "until Sunday at midnight."].
>
> [The offer — what % off, what's included, what's excluded.]
>
> [Why — the reason. A season, a milestone, a "I just need to make room for the new collection."]
>
> [The ask — "Shop the sale" or "DM me 'SALE' for the link."]
>
> → Tag a friend who [would love this / deserves a treat / is rebuilding their wardrobe].

**Worked example:**
> *Spring sale. 48 hours only.*
>
> *20% off the entire shop, including the new Linen Collection. Excludes gift cards.*
>
> *Why? I'm closing the studio for two weeks at the end of the month and I need to make room for the new fall line. So: discount, deep breath, see you in May.*
>
> *Shop the sale: link in bio. Code SPRING20 at checkout.*
>
> *→ Tag a friend who deserves a new spring tote.*

### P3 — The Customer Story Sale

A testimonial that becomes a sale post. Best for product promos with social proof.

**Structure:**
> Last week, [name] DM'd me this:
>
> "[The actual quote, slightly cleaned up, 1-3 sentences. Should include a result.]"
>
> [One sentence on what [name] bought and how long ago.]
>
> [The offer: "If you've been on the fence, this is your sign."]
>
> [The ask: "DM me 'READY' for the link" or "Tap the product tag to see [name]'s exact order."]
>
> → Save this if you've been thinking about it.

**Worked example:**
> *Last week, Sarah DM'd me this:*
>
> *"I bought the Linen Tote in November. It's been through 4 countries, a beach, a rained-on farmer's market, and a toddler. It looks better now than the day I got it."*
>
> *Sarah has had hers for 5 months.*
>
> *If you've been on the fence, this is your sign. $58, free shipping this week, lifetime repair guarantee.*
>
> *→ Save this if you've been thinking about it. Tap the product tag to see Sarah's exact tote.*

### P4 — The Restock Alert

For when a sold-out item is back. The scarcity is already real.

**Structure:**
> It's back. The [product] you [verb — DMed me about / asked for / stalked the site for].
>
> [Time scarcity — "Last time it sold out in [time period]. I made [number] this time. That's it."]
>
> [One detail about what makes this batch special — new color, slight tweak, first time in this size.]
>
> [The ask — "Shop the restock" or "Tag your friend who missed it last time."]
>
> → Set your alarm. This is the last restock of [season].

**Worked example:**
> *It's back. The Olive Linen Tote — the one you DMed me about for 6 weeks.*
>
> *Last time it sold out in 4 days. I made 40 this time. That's it.*
>
> *This batch is slightly improved: leather handles are an inch longer (y'all asked), and I added the hidden zip pocket on the inside.*
>
> *$58. Link in bio. Set your alarm.*
>
> *→ Tag a friend who missed the last drop.*

### P5 — The "Why I Made It" Launch Story

For brand-new products with a real origin story. Ties the product to the founder's voice.

**Structure:**
> I almost didn't launch this.
>
> [Why — the doubt, the obstacle, the "who am I to make this."]
>
> Then [the trigger — a customer, a moment, a conversation].
>
> [What the product is, what it does, who it's for.]
>
> [The pricing. The link. The launch details.]
>
> → If you've been waiting for [the thing this product solves], this is it.

**Worked example:**
> *I almost didn't launch this.*

> *I've been making candles for 6 years. I knew exactly what a good candle smelled like, and I kept telling myself the market was "saturated" and there was no room for another one.*

> *Then last March, a customer emailed: "I just want a candle that doesn't make me feel guilty about the ingredients."*

For "buy 2 get 1" or bundle deals. Reduces the per-item sticker shock.

>
> [The ask — "Tap the product tag" or "Link in bio. Ships Friday."]
>
> → Best for [use case — gifting / your first order / restocking the basics].

**Worked example:**
> *The Starter Kit — the smart way to begin.*
>
> *• The Linen Tote — $58*
> *• The Wallet Pouch — $32*
> *• The Keychain — $18*
>

> *This is the kit I wish I had on day one of my business. Three things that work together, last forever, and get better with use.*
>
> *→ Best for your first order, or for gifting someone who just started something new.*

## Part 3 — Engagement Captions (10)

These captions exist to drive comments, saves, and shares. They are designed to feel like a conversation, not a broadcast.

### G1 — The "This or That"

The lowest-friction engagement. People answer without thinking. The algorithm sees a flood of comments and surfaces the post to more people.

**Structure:**
> [This or that]?
>
> [Option A] or [Option B]?
>
> [Optional: a tiny bit of context or a one-line defense of one option.]
>
> → No wrong answer. Genuinely curious.

**Worked example:**
> *Quick one:*
>
> *Early bird launch or quiet, surprise drop?*
>
> *I'm planning the Fall Collection and I genuinely cannot decide. Tell me which one you'd actually want to see.*
>
> *→ No wrong answer. Genuinely curious.*

### G2 — The "Tell Me About You"

Use this on "Meet Me" intro posts. Gets the audience to introduce themselves in the comments.

**Structure:**
> Hi, I'm [name] — [one-line identity, the kind that fits in a sentence].
>
> I [do the thing] for [the audience] and I've been doing it for [time].
>
> Quick question: [the question that gets them to share a small personal detail].
>
> → Tell me in the comments. I'll be replying all day.

**Worked example:**
> *Hi, I'm Jane — I help handmade sellers turn weekend side-hustles into full-time businesses.*
>
> *I've been doing it for 6 years, full-time for 3.*
>
> *Quick question: what's the one thing you wish you knew before you started selling?*
>
> *→ Tell me in the comments. I'll be replying all day.*

### G3 — The "Unpopular Opinion"

Takes a stance. The comments will follow. Use sparingly — once a month max — to keep the edge.

**Structure:**
> Unpopular opinion: [the opinion, stated as a confident one-liner].
>
> [The defense, in 2-3 sentences. Acknowledge the other side exists. Stand by yours.]
>
> [Optional: a "change my mind" closer.]
>
> → Tell me if I'm wrong. Or tell me if I'm right and you've been thinking it too.

**Worked example:**
> *Unpopular opinion: most "passive income" advice is just active income with extra steps.*
>
> *I have a digital product that made $14K last year. I also worked 40 hours a week maintaining, updating, marketing, and customer-supporting it. There was nothing passive about it.*
>
> *The thing is — I'm not mad about it. I love the work. I just wish someone had been honest about the 40 hours.*
>
> *→ Tell me if I'm wrong. Or tell me if I'm right and you've been thinking it too.*

### G4 — The "Complete the Sentence"

Easy engagement. The brain finishes the sentence before it decides to comment.

**Structure:**
> Finish the sentence:
>
> "The best [topic] advice I ever got was ___."
>
> Mine: [one-line answer, personal].
>
> → Drop yours in the comments. I'll feature my favorites in stories.

**Worked example:**
> *Finish the sentence:*
>
> *"The best pricing advice I ever got was ___."*
>
> *Mine: "Charge what you'd want to be paid if you were the customer." — said by a mentor in 2019, still true.*
>
> *→ Drop yours in the comments. I'll feature my favorites in stories this Friday.*

### G5 — The Poll Question

For when you want simple binary engagement. Pairs with Instagram's poll sticker on stories.

**Structure:**
> Quick poll:
>
> [The question — binary, "this or that" style].
>
> A) [Option A]
> B) [Option B]
>
> [Optional: "Vote in stories, tell me why in the comments."]
>
> → Curious what the split is.

**Worked example:**
> *Quick poll for the makers:*
>
> *Where do you spend the most time each week?*
>
> *A) Making the thing*
> *B) Selling the thing*
>
> *Be honest. I'm at about 60/40 (B), and I'm trying to flip it.*
>
> *→ Curious what the split is. Drop a letter in the comments.*

### G6 — The "What Should I Post"

Drives engagement AND builds a content calendar at the same time.

**Structure:**
> Help me pick: which one should I post next?
>
> A) [Idea 1, in one line]
> B) [Idea 2, in one line]
> C) [Idea 3, in one line]
>
> Whichever gets the most votes goes live [timeframe].
>
