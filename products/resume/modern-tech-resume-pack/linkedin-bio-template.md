# LinkedIn Bio Template — Match Your Resume Style

A matching LinkedIn "About" + Headline template, paired to each of the 5 resume styles. LinkedIn is the second-most-parsed document after the resume — recruiters and hiring managers will read it within 5 seconds of finding the buyer.

---

## Why LinkedIn Matters (and Why Most Are Bad)

Three problems with the average tech LinkedIn:

1. **The "About" is a paragraph of adjectives.** "Passionate engineer with a proven track record of delivering high-quality solutions." Recruiters skim it and move on.
2. **The headline is a job title.** "Senior Software Engineer at Stripe" — boring, undifferentiated, and misses the chance to surface the buyer's specific edge.
3. **The bullets under the headline (the Featured section) are empty or random.** The buyer wastes the prime real estate above the fold.

The fix: write the headline and About like the resume's executive summary. Specific. Outcome-driven. Voice-aware.

## Featured Section (the bullets under the headline)

LinkedIn's Featured section is prime real estate. The buyer should populate it with:

- **1 link to their personal site or portfolio** (the best single asset)
- **1 link to a public artifact** — a talk, a paper, a blog post, an OSS repo
- **1 link to a press feature** — TechCrunch, Forbes, a podcast
- **1 link to a recent project** — a side project, a launch announcement, a recent talk

Order: most impressive first.

---

## Experience Section — Match the Resume

The LinkedIn Experience section should mirror the resume but in a different format. LinkedIn allows multi-line descriptions with rich text. The buyer should:

1. **Use the same role titles and dates as the resume.** No inflation, no abbreviation.
2. **Expand the bullets.** LinkedIn is more permissive than a 1-page resume. Add 5-7 bullets per role, not 3-5.
3. **Add media.** Each role can have images, slides, links. The buyer should add 1-2 artifacts per recent role (a screenshot, a public dashboard, a talk slide deck).
4. **Match the resume's voice.** The buyer should rewrite the resume bullets in the LinkedIn voice — slightly more conversational, slightly longer.

## Skills Section — Curate, Don't Dump

LinkedIn's Skills section is a minefield. The algorithm uses it to match the buyer to recruiter searches.

**Best practice:**
- Add 25-50 skills (not the maximum 50)
- Pin the 3 most relevant skills to the top
- Use the exact terms recruiters search: "Distributed Systems" not "Building distributed stuff"; "React" not "React.js"
- Endorse the skills of others (they endorse back)

**For engineers:** Languages, frameworks, systems, infra, domain specialties. Match the JD terms.
**For designers:** Tools, methods, domains. Match the JD terms.
**For PMs:** Skills (roadmapping, A/B testing, user research), tools (Amplitude, Mixpanel, Looker), domains.
**For founders:** Domains, methods, specializations.

## Recommendations — Strategic, Not Organic

The buyer should aim for 5-10 recommendations, weighted toward:
- 2-3 from managers (the highest credibility signal)
- 2-3 from peers (IC-to-IC, signals respect)
- 1-2 from reports (signals "I grew people")
- 1 from a customer or cross-functional partner (signals "I ship through others")

The buyer should *write* recommendations for others (which often prompts them to write one back). The buyer should *not* write their own recommendations on their profile.

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

❌ "Passionate engineer with a proven track record of delivering high-quality solutions."
✅ "Staff engineer on Stripe's data platform team. 40B events/day, Rust + Kafka."

❌ "Open to opportunities" in the headline (signals desperation, hurts negotiating leverage).
✅ "Open to principal/staff IC roles at companies where the data plane is the product."

❌ "Thoughts?" as the closer on every post.
✅ Specific questions or claims that invite specific responses.

❌ Empty Featured section.
✅ 4 curated links to the buyer's best work.

❌ "CEO & Founder, Visionary Leader, Disruptor" in the headline.
✅ "Founder & CEO, Lattice (Acq. Stripe '24) • ex-Airbnb • YC W19."
